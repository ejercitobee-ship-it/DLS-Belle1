## 1. Before You Begin — What You'll Need

- A quality **digital hygrometer** — *not included; purchase separately.* A calibrated one is strongly recommended.
- **Distilled water only** — never tap water (minerals cause mold and damage)
- A **humidification device** (bead, gel, or electronic) or a **69–72% RH humidity pouch** — *not included; purchase separately*
- A soft, clean, lint-free cloth
- Patience — seasoning takes **7 to 14 days**. Do not rush it.

⚠️ **Never pour or spray water directly onto the Spanish cedar interior.** This warps the wood, raises the grain, and can cause splitting or mold. Always humidify indirectly.

---

## 2. Seasoning Your Humidor (First-Time Setup)

New Spanish cedar is dry and will pull moisture away from your cigars until the wood is properly conditioned. Seasoning charges the wood so it holds a stable humidity.

**Recommended method — the pouch (safest):**

1. Wipe the interior gently with a cloth *lightly* dampened with distilled water — damp, not wet. Do not soak.
2. Place a **69–72% RH humidification pouch** inside, close the lid, and leave it undisturbed.
3. Add your calibrated hygrometer.
4. Wait **7–14 days**, checking every couple of days, until the interior stabilizes at your target humidity (typically **68–72% RH**).
5. Once stable, install your permanent humidification device and begin storing cigars.

**Alternative — the shot-glass method (experienced users):**
Place a small dish or shot glass of distilled water inside next to (not touching) your humidification device, close the lid, and let it condition for several days, refilling as needed until humidity holds steady. Remove the dish before loading cigars.

> **Do not store cigars until the Humidor has fully seasoned and holds a stable reading.** Loading cigars into an unseasoned humidor will dry them out.

---

## 3. Daily & Ongoing Care

- **Target range:** Keep interior humidity at **65–72% RH** and temperature around **65–70°F (18–21°C)**. The classic guideline is "70/70."
- **Check the hygrometer** every few days at first, then weekly once you know your Humidor's rhythm.
- **Refill / recharge** your humidification device **with distilled water only**, before it runs dry — typically every 1–4 weeks depending on climate and device.
- **Rotate your cigars** occasionally so they age evenly.
- **Open sparingly.** Every opening exchanges humidity. In dry climates, keep openings brief.

---

## 4. Seasonal & Climate Adjustments

- **Dry / winter / air-conditioned environments:** the wood loses moisture faster — check and refill more often; consider a higher-output device.
- **Humid / summer environments:** watch for over-humidification (see below).
- **Keep the Humidor away from** direct sunlight, heating and cooling vents, exterior walls, and any heat source. Sunlight and heat swings are among the most common causes of cracking and finish damage — and are **not covered by warranty**.

---

## 5. What NOT to Do (These Void Your Warranty)

- ❌ **Do not use tap, filtered, or spring water** — distilled water only.
- ❌ **Do not spray or pour water on the cedar** — always humidify indirectly.
- ❌ **Do not over-humidify.** Above ~75% RH invites mold and tobacco beetles. If you see fuzzy white/green growth or tiny holes in cigars, act immediately (Section 6).
- ❌ **Do not place the Humidor in a refrigerator, freezer, or in direct sun.**
- ❌ **Do not use household cleaners, polishes, or solvents** on the interior or exterior. Wipe the exterior with a dry or barely-damp soft cloth only.
- ❌ **Do not modify, drill, refinish, or repair** the Humidor yourself — unauthorized modification voids the warranty.

## 6. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Humidity won't rise / cigars drying out | Wood not fully seasoned, or device dry | Re-season; refill device with distilled water |
| Humidity too high (>75%) | Over-humidification | Remove/reduce humidification, crack the lid briefly, monitor |
| White/green fuzzy growth (mold) | Excess moisture / water contact | Remove cigars, gently wipe affected area with a cloth lightly dampened with distilled water, dry, and lower humidity |
| Tiny holes in cigars / small beetles | Tobacco beetle (from heat/humidity) | Freeze affected cigars per standard beetle protocol; inspect and stabilize humidity |
| Musty smell | Poor air circulation or early mold | Rotate cigars, reduce humidity, ensure device isn't oversaturated |

If a problem appears to stem from a defect in materials or workmanship rather than care or environment, contact us (Section 7) within your warranty period.
