## Humidor types explained

Use this to help a customer pick the right style.

- **Desktop humidors.** Tabletop boxes, typically holding 20 to 150 cigars. Best for personal collections and gifts. They rely on a passive humidification device and a hygrometer, and they look at home on a desk or shelf.
- **Cabinet humidors.** Furniture grade, floor standing or large freestanding units that can hold hundreds to a few thousand cigars. Best for growing collections and for aging cigars over time. Many use Spanish cedar shelves and drawers.
- **Electronic humidors.** Desktop or cabinet units with an active, digital humidification system that adds or holds moisture automatically. Best for customers who want set and hold consistency with less hands on maintenance.
- **Travel humidors.** Compact, hard sided, sealed cases, often holding 5 to 20 cigars. Best for protecting cigars on trips. They keep humidity stable for shorter periods.
- **Coolerdors and wineadors.** A cooler or a wine fridge converted into cigar storage, popular with hobbyists for high capacity on a budget. We can advise, though our focus is finished humidors and bespoke builds.
- **Walk in humidors and cigar rooms.** Fully climate controlled rooms for serious collectors and businesses. This is our bespoke specialty.

## How a humidor works, and why Spanish cedar

A humidor is a sealed wooden box or cabinet designed to hold a stable relative humidity so cigars stay in perfect condition. Three parts do the work:

1. **A good seal.** The lid or door closes snugly so moisture stays in and outside air stays out.
2. **A humidification device.** It releases moisture slowly to keep the air inside at the target humidity.
3. **A hygrometer.** It measures the relative humidity so you can confirm conditions are right.

Most fine humidors are lined with **Spanish cedar** (botanically a mahogany, Cedrela odorata). It matters for several reasons: it absorbs and releases moisture to buffer humidity swings, its aroma complements cigars, it helps deter tobacco beetles, and it aids the slow aging of cigars. This is why we build with kiln dried Spanish cedar.

## Choosing the right size

A useful rule: a humidor holds its stated capacity only when packed tight with one common cigar size. In real life, mixed sizes, boxes, and airflow mean you should plan for roughly **half to two thirds** of the stated number. Buy for where your collection is heading, not just where it is today.

- Under about 25 cigars: a small desktop humidor or a travel case.
- 25 to 100 cigars: a mid size desktop humidor.
- 100 to several hundred: a large desktop or a cabinet humidor.
- Several hundred and growing, or aging cigars long term: a cabinet, or a bespoke build.

## Seasoning a new humidor

A new wooden humidor must be seasoned before you store cigars in it. The dry Spanish cedar will pull moisture out of your cigars until the wood is conditioned, so seasoning charges the wood first. Skipping this step is the most common reason new humidors will not hold humidity.

**Method 1, the gentle way (recommended, using humidification packs):**
1. Wipe nothing with tap water. Place one or two calibrated two way humidification packs (for example 69 to 72% packs) inside.
2. Close the lid and leave it for one to two weeks, checking that the interior settles near the target humidity.
3. Once it holds steady, add your cigars.

**Method 2, the dish method:**
1. Place a small dish of distilled water inside, and stand your humidification device separately. Do not let cigars touch water.
2. Close the lid for 24 hours, then check. Repeat for two to three days until the interior holds near the target.
3. Remove the dish, confirm the reading is stable, then add your cigars.

Never wipe the interior with tap water, and never rush it. Use distilled water only. Seasoning a large cabinet takes longer than a small desktop. For a specific unit, follow its manual, and we are glad to help.

## Ideal humidity and temperature

The classic target many collectors use is around **70% relative humidity and about 70°F**, the so called 70/70 rule. In practice a healthy range is roughly **65 to 72% relative humidity** and **65 to 70°F**.

A few refinements worth sharing:
- Many experienced collectors keep humidity slightly lower, near **65 to 68%**, which many find gives a better draw and burn and adds a margin against mold.
- Keep temperature at or below about **70°F**. Tobacco beetles can hatch in warm, humid conditions, and the risk rises above roughly **72°F**, so cooler is safer.
- Aim for **stable** conditions above all. Slow, steady is better than chasing an exact number. Avoid direct sunlight, heat sources, and damp basements.

Personal preference matters, so it is fine to tell customers to settle in their preferred range and keep it consistent.

## Hygrometers, and how to calibrate them

A hygrometer measures relative humidity. Two kinds:
- **Analog (dial).** Attractive and classic, but often less accurate and needs calibration.
- **Digital.** Generally more accurate and easier to read. Recommended for anyone who wants confidence in the number.

**The salt test, to calibrate any hygrometer:**
1. Put a teaspoon of table salt in a bottle cap and add a few drops of water to make a damp paste, do not flood it.
2. Seal the cap and the hygrometer together in a zip top bag or airtight container.
3. Wait six hours at stable room temperature. A correct hygrometer will read **75%**.
4. If it reads high or low, note the difference and either adjust the calibration screw (analog) or remember the offset (digital).

Calibrating once or twice a year keeps your readings honest.

## Humidification methods

Different ways to add and hold moisture:

- **Two way humidification packs.** Sealed packs that both add and remove moisture to hold a set humidity (for example 65%, 69%, or 72%). The easiest and most consistent option for most desktop humidors. Replace when they stiffen.
- **Humidity beads or crystals (acrylic polymer).** Refillable media that release moisture. Recharge with distilled water as needed.
- **Traditional foam or floral foam devices.** The classic sponge style unit. Use only distilled water or a propylene glycol solution, never tap water, to avoid mold and mineral buildup.
- **Electronic humidification systems.** Active units that maintain humidity in cabinets and walk ins. Set the target and the system holds it. Follow the unit's manual for filling and settings.

Whatever the method, use **distilled water** or a proper propylene glycol solution, never tap water.

## Everyday care and maintenance

- **Check the hygrometer regularly,** weekly is a good habit, and keep your humidification device charged or filled.
- **Refill with distilled water only.** Tap water invites mold and mineral deposits.
- **Rotate your cigars** every so often so they age evenly, moving lower cigars up and back cigars forward.
- **Do not overpack.** Cigars need a little airflow. Overpacking blocks even humidification.
- **Keep it closed.** Every time you open it, humidity escapes. Open, choose, close.
- **Keep it out of sunlight and away from heat and vents.** Stable temperature is your friend.
- **Give a new or long unused humidor a re season** if humidity keeps dropping.

## Common problems and how to fix them

- **Humidity keeps dropping.** Usually the humidor was not fully seasoned, the seal is weak, or the device needs refilling or is undersized. Re season, refill with distilled water, and check the seal.
- **Humidity too high, above about 75%.** Remove the water source briefly, crack the lid for short periods, and let it settle. Persistent high humidity risks mold.
- **Mold versus plume.** Plume (also called bloom) is a fine, dusty white crystalline that brushes off and is harmless. Mold is fuzzy, often blue green, and may smell musty. If in doubt, isolate the affected cigars and contact us. Prevent both with stable humidity near 65 to 68% and clean, distilled water.
- **Cigars feel dry or cracked.** The environment ran too dry, often from skipping seasoning or a weak seal. Bring humidity up slowly over days, not all at once, to avoid splitting the wrappers.
- **Uneven or wet spots.** Do not let cigars touch the humidification device or any water. Reposition the device and rotate cigars.
- **Tobacco beetles.** Tiny holes in cigars and fine dust can mean beetles, which hatch in warm, humid conditions. Freeze affected cigars in a sealed bag as a rescue step, keep storage below about 70°F, and reach out if you need help.

For anything you are unsure about, especially anything that could affect a valuable collection, connect the customer with our team.

## Glossary of terms

- **Relative humidity (RH).** The percentage of moisture in the air, the key number for cigar storage.
- **Seasoning.** Conditioning a new humidor's cedar with moisture before storing cigars.
- **Spanish cedar.** The preferred humidor lining wood, prized for moisture buffering and aroma.
- **Hygrometer.** The gauge that measures relative humidity.
- **Two way humidification.** A pack or system that both adds and removes moisture to hold a set humidity.
- **Plume or bloom.** Harmless crystalline that can appear on well aged cigars, brushes off.
- **Mold.** Fuzzy growth from excess or unstable humidity, a problem to address.
- **Coolerdor or wineador.** A cooler or wine fridge converted for cigar storage.
- **Propylene glycol (PG) solution.** A humidification solution that helps hold humidity near 70% and resists mold.
